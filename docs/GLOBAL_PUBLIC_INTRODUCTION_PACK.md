## 2. Capability boundary — mandatory wording

The repository contains design documents, tokenomics, contract specifications, backend/database source components, tests and deployment plans. Their presence does not prove a live product or successful production operation.

| Subject | What may be stated | What must not be claimed |
|---|---|---|
| TON / Jetton | “Designed for development on TON using the Jetton standard (TEP-74).” | “VCOIN is live on TON”, unless the exact Master address, network and on-chain state are independently verified and published. |
| Supply | “The approved design specifies a proposed initial supply of 2.1 billion VCOIN and six decimals.” | “10 billion tokens have been minted, issued, locked or allocated on-chain.” |
| Minting | “The design requires no additional minting after the one-time initial issuance.” | “Minting is permanently disabled” until the deployed contract and its state are independently verified. |
| Mining | “Wallet-based mining is a planned capability with a documented specification.” | “Mining is live”, “users are earning”, or any claim that internal pending balances are wallet-held tokens. |
| Wallet / Telegram | “Wallet ownership verification and Telegram integration are in the technical plan.” | “Wallet connection, user verification or Telegram extraction is operational” without an end-to-end test record. |
| Price | Do not include the internal modelled reference-price schedule in public introductions. If discussed in a technical context, label it a non-market simulation. | Any market-price forecast, automatic appreciation promise, guaranteed floor, liquidity promise or expected return. |
| Security / audit | “Security review and independent audit are release gates.” | “Audited”, “fully secure”, “risk-free” or “approved” without a named, published, independent report and scope. |
| Partnerships / listings | Name a partner only after a signed agreement, partner approval to disclose, and legal review. | Any implied TON Foundation, wallet-provider, exchange, regulator, bank or institutional endorsement without written evidence. |
| Adoption | “The project is preparing documentation for review.” | “Globally accepted”, “used worldwide”, “available in every country” or invented user/partner numbers. |

**Current release gate:** no public token offer, token-sale solicitation, paid token promotion, exchange/listing claim, real-value payout, public mining activation or Mainnet launch is authorised by this document. Keep the mining endpoint fail-closed until wallet-based mining and payout reconciliation are implemented and verified.

## 7. Trust, security, transparency and founder privacy

Public founder anonymity must never mean anonymous legal accountability. Before any public token offer or regulated service, the responsible legal entity, jurisdiction, service operator, contact route and legally required beneficial-ownership information must be available to competent authorities, regulated counterparties and independent auditors as required by law.

Public disclosures should include, at minimum:
- Current project status and a dated list of live versus planned capabilities.
- Token design parameters clearly labelled as proposed until verified on-chain.
- Allocation policy and vesting/release rules once implemented and independently reconciled.
- Treasury governance, approval thresholds, key-management controls and periodic aggregate reporting. Confidential personal identity and sensitive wallet-control details need not be published where law permits, but must be available to authorised reviewers.
- Independent smart-contract audit scope, report date, findings and remediation status once completed.
- Security incident, vulnerability disclosure and user-complaint procedures.
- Material risks: total loss, volatility, smart-contract bugs, wallet compromise, irreversible transfers, provider outage, liquidity absence, regulatory changes and tax obligations.
- A clear statement that no regulator, exchange, wallet provider or TON ecosystem organisation endorses the project unless written evidence supports that exact statement.

Do not collect seed phrases or private keys. Do not ask users to send tokens to an address to “activate” a wallet. Do not represent internal balances as on-chain assets.
